# T&M Tree Service — Page Profit Check Audit

## Quick take
T&M Tree Service is a good fit for a **Solution A surgical homepage cleanup** because the current site already has recognizable brand material, local service proof, and a direct contact path — but the first-screen decision flow can be made clearer without making the business feel redesigned.

**Recommendation:** preserve the current site family and rebuild the homepage around one clear primary action: **Request a Free Tree Estimate**.

**Visual resemblance:** High  
**Audit coverage target:** High

## Site fingerprint
- **Logo/brand assets:** Business-name treatment preserved in a cleaner header.
- **Dominant colors:** #244a2f, #8cbf3f, #f4f7ef, #102416.
- **Typography feel:** Practical local-service sans-serif body copy with stronger serif-style headings for hierarchy.
- **Header/nav pattern:** Phone/contact-forward local service navigation with fewer competing choices.
- **Hero/image treatment:** Same contractor/service-business positioning, but with a clearer estimate or schedule action.
- **Section rhythm:** Hero, trust strip, service cards, proof band, process block, contact section.
- **Recognizable quirks to preserve:**
- green outdoor/tree-care palette
- family-owned local positioning
- tree removal and pruning wording
- award/review proof
- call-or-text service behavior

## What is already working
- The business has a live website with clear service intent in Lisle / Naperville, IL.
- The site already communicates family owned since 2004.
- The site already communicates tree removal + trimming.
- The site already communicates a+ bbb / award proof.
- The live homepage confirms a relevant service mix: Tree removal, Tree trimming, Stump grinding, Lot clearing.
- There is a direct contact, quote, estimate, appointment, or booking path that can be made easier to notice.

## Biggest conversion issues

1. **Critical:** Several prominent links appear to point to an old staging domain, which can damage trust and send visitors away from the live brand.
   - Prototype response: Keep the green tree-service look while making “Request a Free Tree Estimate” the primary CTA.
2. **High:** Free estimates, awards, BBB/Angi/HomeAdvisor proof, and prompt service are strong but too spread out.
   - Prototype response: Bring awards, BBB, free estimates, and prompt service into a first-screen trust strip.
3. **High:** Tree removal, pruning, stump grinding, and lot clearing need a quicker “which problem do you have?” layout.
   - Prototype response: Group removal, pruning, stump, and lot-clearing services by buyer urgency.
4. **High:** The contact form is useful but buried under long content and service-area/navigation clutter.
   - Prototype response: Move contact expectations and phone/email details into a cleaner quote block.
5. **Medium:** Older formatting, menu density, repeated copy, or service-area sprawl makes the page feel heavier than the actual business value.
   - Prototype response: Remove or replace staging-domain-style links so trust does not leak away.

## Best Page Profit Check improvements
1. Keep the green tree-service look while making “Request a Free Tree Estimate” the primary CTA.
2. Remove or replace staging-domain-style links so trust does not leak away.
3. Bring awards, BBB, free estimates, and prompt service into a first-screen trust strip.
4. Group removal, pruning, stump, and lot-clearing services by buyer urgency.
5. Move contact expectations and phone/email details into a cleaner quote block.

## SEO basics to tighten
- Use a homepage title that combines primary service + location + brand.
- Write a concise meta description around the main service and CTA.
- Keep one clear H1 and use H2s for service categories.
- Add descriptive alt text for brand and service images if implemented in the live CMS.
- Add LocalBusiness/Service schema after the approved copy is finalized.

## Realistic implementation angle
This is a focused homepage cleanup sprint, not a rebrand. The existing site should keep its recognizable look while tightening the top section, CTA hierarchy, service grouping, trust proof, and contact expectations.
