# Pete's POWERHOUSE Window Cleaning & Power Washing — Page Profit Check Audit

## Quick take
Pete's POWERHOUSE Window Cleaning & Power Washing is a good fit for a **Solution A surgical homepage cleanup** because the current site has recognizable brand value and real local-business proof, but the first-screen clarity, trust hierarchy, service grouping, and action path can be tightened without making the business feel redesigned.

**Recommendation:** preserve the current site family and rebuild the homepage around one clear primary action: **Schedule an Appointment**.

**Visual resemblance:** High  
**Audit coverage target:** High

## Site fingerprint
- **Logo/brand assets:** Text/wordmark identity and current business-name treatment preserved in a cleaner header.
- **Dominant colors:** #0b4f8a, #ff8a00, #19b5d1, #0d253f.
- **Typography feel:** Montserrat, Arial Black, sans-serif heading feel with Montserrat, Arial, sans-serif body copy direction.
- **Header/nav pattern:** Local-service navigation with phone/contact emphasis.
- **Hero/image treatment:** water power style visual cue with strong service promise.
- **Section rhythm:** Hero, trust strip, service cards, proof band, process block, contact section.
- **Recognizable quirks to preserve:** Elementor contractor homepage; blue/orange POWERHOUSE palette; top phone bars and social icons; large service tiles/slider; “We Do It All” promise.

## What is already working
- The business has a live website with real local-service intent in Naperville, IL.
- The site already contains useful proof: 35+ years serving Naperville, Residential + commercial, Eco-friendly products.
- The existing visual direction is recognizable enough for Solution A instead of a full redesign.
- There is a direct contact or booking path that can be made easier to notice.

## Biggest conversion issues

1. **Critical:** The first screen competes with carousel-style service slides before the visitor sees one clear scheduling path.
   - Prototype response: Replace carousel clutter with a direct exterior-cleaning promise and schedule CTA.
2. **High:** Two phone numbers plus multiple appointment links make contact options feel scattered.
   - Prototype response: Keep phone options visible but make Schedule an Appointment primary.
3. **High:** Service breadth is strong but the menu is too dense for a first-time homeowner.
   - Prototype response: Group services into three surface/problem paths.
4. **High:** Trust proof like 35+ years, eco-friendly products, free estimates, and care standards is not concentrated near the top.
   - Prototype response: Add a four-point trust strip below hero.
5. **Medium:** Commercial and residential messages appear together without guidance.
   - Prototype response: Clarify that both are served and route by exterior problem first.

## Best Page Profit Check improvements
1. Replace scattered first-screen messaging with a clear local-service promise: “Exterior cleaning in Naperville with one clear way to schedule.”
2. Make **Schedule an Appointment** the primary action in the hero, process section, and contact block.
3. Move trust proof near the top: 35+ years serving Naperville, Residential + commercial, Eco-friendly products, Free estimates.
4. Organize services by buyer intent instead of making visitors decode every page/link.
5. Add a simple “what happens next” flow before the form or contact action.
6. Clean template residue, duplicate text, stale content, or technical form clutter where present.

## SEO basics to tighten
- Use a homepage title that combines primary service + location + brand.
- Write a concise meta description around the main service and CTA.
- Keep one clear H1 and use H2s for service categories.
- Add descriptive alt text for brand and service images if implemented in the live CMS.
- Add LocalBusiness/Service schema after the approved copy is finalized.

## Realistic implementation angle
This is a focused homepage cleanup sprint, not a rebrand. The existing site should keep its recognizable look while tightening the top section, CTA hierarchy, service grouping, trust proof, and contact expectations.
