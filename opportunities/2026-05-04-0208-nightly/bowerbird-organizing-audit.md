## What is already working
- The business has a clear local identity: Fort Wayne, professional home organizing, and Lauren as the owner are all present.
- Real project photos and a customer quote give the page useful authenticity.
- The service menu includes helpful pricing anchors: free phone/virtual consult, $45 in-home consult, starter session from $180, team solution from $480.
- The copy has a strong nonjudgmental tone: “No shaming,” “visual peace,” “declutter with respect,” and “no pressure” are valuable trust signals.
- Contact information is visible in the footer, including email and phone.

## Biggest conversion issues

### Critical
1. **CTA path loops visitors back to the homepage instead of making the next step feel concrete.**  
   Buttons such as “Book Consult,” “Contact Lauren,” and “Get started” point to the homepage URL rather than a visible contact/booking destination. The provided `/contact` source URL also returns a 404. That can create hesitation right at the decision point.

2. **The hero explains the service, but the first actionable offer is not specific enough.**  
   The visitor sees a banner and helpful paragraphs, but the strongest low-friction offer — a free 30-minute phone/virtual consultation — is buried in the menu instead of being obvious above the fold.

### High
3. **Trust proof is present but not framed as a conversion asset near the top.**  
   A testimonial appears early, but the page could more clearly summarize “no shaming,” “projects big or small,” and “owner-led consult” before asking visitors to choose a package.

4. **Services are listed by internal package names instead of buyer task paths.**  
   “Consultations,” “Starter Session,” and “Team Solution” are useful, but first-time visitors need to quickly map those to the actual spaces or situations they care about: pantry, closet, garage, office, pre-move, paperwork, etc.

5. **The contact section asks for convenience preference but does not clearly explain what happens after submitting.**  
   The page says Lauren replies within 24 hours, but the consultation sequence could be clearer: send a note, talk with Lauren, get a plan.

### Medium
6. **Multiple H1-style headings weaken SEO structure.**  
   The live page uses many H1s for section titles. A cleaner structure would keep one main H1 and use H2/H3 for sections.

7. **Minor trust-damaging leftovers and copy issues are visible.**  
   Examples include “Copy of Jamie” in testimonial controls, the `/contact` 404, “Fort Wanye” typo, and the Wix footer credit.

8. **Mobile scanning can be improved.**  
   The existing Wix desktop rhythm is recognizable, but long centered text blocks and stacked menu details need stronger cards, shorter paragraphs, and a persistent-feeling CTA path on smaller screens.

## Best Page Profit Check improvements
- Put the free phone/virtual consult in the hero as the primary action: **“Book a free consult.”**
- Keep the original logo/header/banner structure, but make the first copy block more direct: what Bowerbird organizes, who it helps, and what the visitor should do next.
- Add a near-top trust strip: no shaming, projects big or small, owner-led consultation.
- Rebuild the organizing menu as three scannable project-path cards while preserving the original package names and pricing anchors.
- Add a “what happens next” block before the contact action.
- Correct obvious trust issues lightly: contact links go to the contact section, source `/contact` issue is noted, typo corrected, template/footer residue removed in the cleanup.

## SEO basics to tighten
- Use one primary H1 focused on search intent: “Fort Wayne Professional Home Organizing Service” or similar.
- Use H2s for Reviews, Home Organizing Menu, Meet the Owner, Ready for Clutter Relief, and Why the Bowerbird.
- Keep Fort Wayne, professional organizer, home organizing, decluttering, pantry, closet, garage, office, and move-prep language in natural body copy.
- Add a concise meta description focused on the free consult and local service area.
- Fix the “Fort Wanye” typo on the live page.
- Make the Contact nav link point to a working anchor or working contact page rather than a dead `/contact` route.

## Realistic implementation angle
This can be implemented as a focused Wix cleanup rather than a rebuild:
1. Update hero copy and primary CTA.
2. Add or restyle a small trust strip immediately after the hero.
3. Rework the service menu text/cards using the existing three-column visual rhythm.
4. Add a three-step consultation explanation above the form.
5. Fix contact links/anchors and `/contact` routing.
6. Clean footer/template residue and heading hierarchy.

A realistic cleanup sprint should preserve the live page’s recognizable Wix structure while tightening content, hierarchy, and CTA links.
