# TLC Bookkeeping Services LLC — Page Profit Check Audit

## Quick take
TLC has the essentials online: business name, phone, email, address, a simple “Your Business Deserves TLC” positioning line, and a clear QuickBooks/bookkeeping category. The main opportunity is conversion clarity. The current Wix page is very short and credible enough to keep, but it does not quickly answer who TLC helps, what services are available, why to trust the business, or what a visitor should do next.

## What is already working
- Phone number and email are visible near the top and repeated in the contact section.
- The brand name and “TLC” message are memorable for bookkeeping.
- The page is simple, low-friction, and locally anchored in Cape Canaveral / Space Coast.
- QuickBooks Accounting is stated, which gives the service a recognizable buyer need.
- There is a Facebook link for additional validation.

## Biggest conversion issues
### Critical
1. **Hero headline is mostly brand-focused, not outcome-focused.** “Feel the difference” does not tell a rushed small-business owner what help they can get.
   - Prototype fix: keep the same large all-caps hero style, but add a clear subheadline: personalized bookkeeping help so books stay clear, current, and decision-ready.
2. **Primary call to action is vague.** “Get in Touch” appears, but the page does not specify whether a prospect should request a consultation, call, or email.
   - Prototype fix: add a specific “Request a bookkeeping consultation” CTA plus a direct call button.

### High
3. **Services are not packaged.** Visitors see “QuickBooks Accounting” and a paragraph, but no scannable service paths.
   - Prototype fix: add three lightweight cards: Monthly Bookkeeping, QuickBooks Support, Cleanup & Organization.
4. **No “what happens next” reassurance.** Professional-service buyers often hesitate if they do not know what the first conversation involves.
   - Prototype fix: add a short 3-step process section.
5. **Wix/template signals feel unfinished.** The live page includes generic Wix banner language and very sparse sections, which can make an active service look neglected.
   - Prototype fix: preserve the Wix/simple-builder feel, but make the page feel intentionally complete.

### Medium
6. SEO title/meta could be more specific to bookkeeping, QuickBooks, Space Coast, and Cape Canaveral.
7. Contact links should use direct `mailto:` and `tel:` behavior everywhere.
8. More trust proof would help later: years in business, certifications, industries served, testimonials, or QuickBooks ProAdvisor details if accurate.

## Best Page Profit Check improvements
- Rewrite the hero to explain the business outcome without changing TLC’s tone.
- Add one specific CTA above the fold.
- Turn the single “Our Way” paragraph into a clearer service explanation.
- Add simple service cards so visitors can self-identify their need.
- Add a “What happens next” section to reduce inquiry hesitation.
- Tighten page title and meta description around “QuickBooks bookkeeping” and “Space Coast, Florida.”

## SEO basics to tighten
- Use a title like: `TLC Bookkeeping Services, LLC | QuickBooks Bookkeeping on Florida's Space Coast`.
- Add a meta description focused on personalized bookkeeping and QuickBooks support.
- Use one clear H1/Hero message and descriptive H2s for services/contact.
- Keep NAP details consistent: TLC Bookkeeping Services, LLC; 8404 Canaveral Blvd., Cape Canaveral, FL 32920; 321-349-9668.
- Add service/location language naturally: bookkeeping, QuickBooks support, Cape Canaveral, Space Coast, Florida.

## Realistic implementation angle
This is a good fit for a surgical cleanup, not a redesign. The existing Wix site can keep its simple centered layout, pale blue/white feel, big uppercase hero, and minimal contact-forward structure. Improvements can be made by editing copy and adding a few sections in Wix: CTA buttons, service cards, and a next-step section.

## Offer fit
Strong fit for a $99 Page Profit Check because the business already has a live site and clear contact info, but likely loses inquiries from vague positioning and sparse service packaging. A small prototype gives the owner something concrete to evaluate without asking for a full rebuild.
