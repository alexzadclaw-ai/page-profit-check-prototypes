## What is already working
- The site clearly names the business and provides direct email and phone contact information.
- “No Job Too Small” is a simple promise that fits a handyman buyer’s mindset.
- The homepage includes real project photos: deck build/stain, cross-pattern fence, and fence back section.
- The service list is specific enough to prove breadth: fencing, painting, deck staining, flooring, windows/sliding doors, minor electrical, deck repair, and minor plumbing.
- The visual identity is simple but memorable: black/white/yellow, all-caps headings, and a practical one-page layout.

## Biggest conversion issues

1. **Critical:** The first screen is dominated by the business name, but the action path is weak; there is no obvious above-the-fold button to call, email, or request help.
   - Prototype response: Add a clear hero CTA cluster: **Get in Touch About Your Project**, phone, and email while preserving the split hero.
2. **Critical:** The Wix ad banner, Wix social links, and “Proudly created with Wix.com” footer residue hurt trust for a service business.
   - Prototype response: Remove Wix residue from the static prototype and replace the footer/contact area with business-specific contact details.
3. **High:** The site mentions student/rental-property experience, but it does not quickly translate that into buyer reassurance about quality, communication, and project fit.
   - Prototype response: Reframe the same public facts into top trust points: since 2015, rental-property repair experience, direct owner communication, and “No Job Too Small.”
4. **High:** The project cards have photos, but the CTAs all say “Get in Touch” without telling visitors what kind of work they should ask about.
   - Prototype response: Keep the three project cards but add short context and more specific inquiry labels.
5. **High:** The service list is long and vertically dense, so visitors may not quickly see which category matches their problem.
   - Prototype response: Group services into outdoor/deck/fence, interior repairs/painting/flooring, and small electrical/plumbing/window tasks while retaining the original item names.
6. **Medium:** The contact section is sparse and has a large blank feel; it does not explain what happens after someone reaches out.
   - Prototype response: Add a simple three-step “send the job details → confirm scope → schedule the work” flow before final contact details.

## Best Page Profit Check improvements
1. Keep the Wix one-page structure but make the hero action-oriented: service promise, core proof, email, phone, and a direct CTA.
2. Preserve real project photos and add better labels so they function as trust proof, not just a gallery.
3. Move the strongest confidence signals directly below the hero: since 2015, rental-property experience, direct communication, and no-job-too-small flexibility.
4. Reorganize the services into clearer buyer paths while keeping the original service names.
5. Replace Wix/social-template residue with a clean, business-specific contact/footer section.
6. Add a “what happens next” block so visitors know how to start a repair conversation.

## SEO basics to tighten
