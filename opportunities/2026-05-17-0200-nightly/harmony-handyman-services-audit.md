# Harmony Handyman Services Page Profit Check Audit

## Quick take

The current root page opens as a Wix contact page with a strong handyman background, real phone/email links, clear service-area intent, and practical instructions to send project details. The main opportunity is to make the first screen behave more like a local-service conversion page: show what Harmony handles, prove it serves San Jose/Silicon Valley, and make calling or emailing project photos the obvious next step.

## What is already working

- The page already has a practical, contact-first angle: phone, email, form, mailing address, service areas, and instructions to include name, phone, location, availability, project description, and photos.
- The broader site has useful trust material: "Proudly serving the Bay Area since 2013," real testimonials, a long services list, and a large gallery of original project photos.
- The visual identity is recognizable: wood/tools background, colorful Harmony logo, red/cyan brand title, boxed Wix layout, tabbed navigation, and direct handyman language.
- Phone and email are clickable on the contact page.
- The business already tells visitors that pictures help, which is exactly the right behavior for handyman quote requests.

## Biggest conversion issues

- The first screen on the root/contact page says "CONTACT US" but does not immediately answer "what do you do?" or "should I call or email photos?"
- The visible phone number is not promoted as the primary action, even though emergency/small repair visitors often want to call first.
- Services are buried on another page. A visitor landing on Contact has to infer that Harmony handles plumbing, electrical fixtures, drywall, painting, carpentry, appliances, and small renovations.
- The form feels generic and the Send button is visually weak.
- The service-area list is useful but long and hard to scan.
- Trust cues are not close enough to the contact action. Testimonials, since-2013 proof, gallery photos, and service coverage should support the form and phone CTA.
- The Wix banner and hidden/duplicated navigation reduce professionalism.
- Several text issues hurt polish: "Testimonals," "Employment Oportunities," "Cervices," and some awkward wording in the About/services copy.

## Best Page Profit Check improvements

- Turn the top contact page into a request path: headline, services summary, phone CTA, email CTA, and clear project-photo instructions.
- Add a compact "Common projects" row near the top: plumbing repairs, fixture installs, drywall/painting, doors/trim, appliance installs, small renovations.
- Add a trust strip: San Jose/Silicon Valley, serving since 2013, photos requested for faster response, testimonials/gallery available.
- Keep the current Wix/handyman feel but clean up spacing, hierarchy, and button sizing.
- Move the long area list into a tighter service-area panel instead of letting it dominate the contact column.
- Keep the existing contact form idea, but add labels that match real quote information: name, phone/email, city, project details, photos reminder.
- Use original gallery photos as small proof cards instead of generic stock imagery.

## SEO basics to tighten

- Make the root page title more service/location focused, for example: "San Jose Handyman Services | Harmony Handyman Services."
- Use one visible H1 on the root page that includes both service and location.
- Add concise intro copy mentioning handyman services, home repairs, general maintenance, painting, drywall, fixture installation, and San Jose/Silicon Valley.
- Fix spelling issues in navigation and body copy.
- Add descriptive alt text to original project photos and logo.
- Add local business schema later if the Wix setup allows it.
- Make phone, email, hours, service area, and primary services consistent across footer/contact pages.

## Realistic implementation angle

This does not need a full rebuild. The practical path is a surgical Wix page refresh:

1. Keep the existing logo, background, nav style, colors, and boxed layout.
2. Rework the Contact/root first screen so it explains services and gives two obvious actions: call or email project photos.
3. Pull short trust snippets from About, Testimonials, Services, and Gallery into the contact page.
4. Tighten the form labels and service-area presentation.
5. Clean up spelling, page titles, headings, and image alt text.

## Offer fit

Good fit for a $99 Page Profit Check because the site has enough existing material to diagnose and show a before/after direction quickly. The $99 should stay positioned as audit/diagnosis only: review the page, identify highest-impact fixes, and show a visual direction. Implementation would be a separate Wix cleanup project after they approve the direction.
