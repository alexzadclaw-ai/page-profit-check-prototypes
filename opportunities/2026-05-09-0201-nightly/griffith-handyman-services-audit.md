# Griffith Handyman Services - Page Profit Check Audit

## Quick take
Griffith Handyman Services has a real, simple local-service site with working contact information, strong handyman imagery, and a clear service area in Riverbank, California. The biggest opportunity is not a redesign. It is a trust cleanup: remove Wix/template leftovers, make the first screen say what jobs are handled, make the CTA callable, and put proof/next steps closer to the top.

Visual fingerprint observed: Wix-built one-page feel; full-width dark hero using a tools photo; white Poppins-style business name; Raleway-style navigation and body copy; black/charcoal background; coral-red accent (#ff4b39) for headings and CTAs; simple Home/Contact nav; three image service blocks; quote/contact section with handyman image; footer still says created with Wix.

## What is already working
- The business name is prominent and memorable.
- The hero uses relevant tool imagery, which fits a handyman brand.
- The phone number and email are visible on the page.
- The copy mentions real service types: fixtures, repairs, fences, painting, mantles, focal pieces, outdoor/lawn work.
- The site states experience since 2014, which is a useful trust signal.
- The tone feels personal and owner-operated, which is good for local handyman leads.

## Biggest conversion issues
| Finding | Severity | Why it hurts |
|---|---:|---|
| Wix ad banner and “Proudly created with Wix.com” are visible. | Critical | These template leftovers make the business look less established and distract from contacting the handyman. |
| Hero only repeats the business name and does not explain the service or area. | Critical | A homeowner landing cold must work too hard to know if this is the right handyman for their project. |
| Primary CTA says “Get An Estimate” but does not expose phone/email on the first screen. | High | Handyman leads often want to call quickly from mobile. The CTA should make the next action obvious. |

| Services are organized as vague questions instead of buyer task paths. | High | Visitors with a broken fence, fixture update, painting job, or custom request need fast matching. |
| No “what happens next” section. | High | A visitor may hesitate if they do not know how estimates, scheduling, or scope discussion works. |
| Mobile has long stacked copy with low scan value. | Medium | The page can feel like a generic template instead of a quick local-service decision page. |
| SEO basics are thin. | Medium | Title and copy do not fully reinforce handyman + Riverbank + task keywords. |
| Some typos/capitalization issues appear in service copy. | Medium | Small errors can reduce perceived professionalism for home-service work. |

## Best Page Profit Check improvements
- Keep the same dark Wix-style visual language, tool imagery, coral accents, and simple structure.
- Replace the generic hero with a clearer service + location statement: reliable fixture updates, home repairs, outdoor jobs, painting, and custom projects for Riverbank area homeowners.
- Add phone and email CTAs in the hero so mobile visitors can act immediately.
- Move the “Since 2014,” “Free Estimates,” and “Riverbank, CA” trust cues directly below the hero.
- Rename service blocks by task path: Fixture Updates, Home Repairs, Design Ideas.
- Add bullets inside each service card so buyers can recognize their job faster.
- Add a three-step “What happens next” section: call/email, get an estimate, schedule the work.
- Remove visible Wix/template leftovers from the customer-facing page.
- Tighten grammar while preserving the owner-operated voice.

## SEO basics to tighten
- Page title should include service and location: “Griffith Handyman Services | Riverbank, CA.”
- Meta description should mention Riverbank handyman services, fixture updates, repairs, painting, outdoor jobs, and free estimates.
- Use one clear H1 with the business name, then descriptive H2/H3 service headings.
- Add service keywords naturally in the hero and service section.
- Make phone links use `tel:` and email links use `mailto:`.
- Add image alt text describing handyman/tools/work scenes.
