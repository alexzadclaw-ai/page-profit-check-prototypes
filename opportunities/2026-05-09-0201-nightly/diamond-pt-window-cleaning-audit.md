# Diamond PT Window Cleaning Audit

## Quick take
Diamond PT Window Cleaning already has the raw ingredients of a trustworthy local service page: a real owner, a real phone number, an email address, a stated Monroe location, licensed and insured language, and reviews that sound genuine. The problem is clarity and polish. The page reads like a Wix single-page build that still needs a pass for hierarchy, service grouping, and stronger call-to-action language.

Visual fingerprint observed: single-page Wix layout, white background, dark green body text, muted olive and tan accents, oversized centered hero headline, one dominant hero image, compact testimonial section, owner bio with portrait, simple contact block, and a form-style submit button at the bottom. The site feels calm and local, but a few template leftovers and loose navigation cues make it feel less finished than it should.

## What is already working
- Real business name and local location are clear: Monroe, WA.
- Phone number and email are visible and clickable.
- The page says fully licensed and insured.
- The owner story is memorable and human.
- Residential and commercial service are both present.
- The reviews are specific enough to feel believable.
- The service mix is valuable: windows, screens, solar panels, roofs, and gutters.

## Biggest conversion issues
### Critical
1. Template leftovers and weak navigation cues make the site feel unfinished. The page includes blank-looking in-page links and a generic bottom submit button without a clearer request path.
   - Prototype fix: remove leftover site-builder noise, replace the loose navigation with meaningful anchors, and make the primary action a specific call or text CTA.

2. The hero is too broad for fast decision-making. It says the business cleans windows, but it does not quickly separate the main jobs or tell visitors what to do next.
   - Prototype fix: tighten the hero headline, add a specific free-quote CTA, and place service buckets directly below the hero.

### High
3. Trust signals are present but too far down the page.
   - Prototype fix: add a trust strip right under the hero with licensed and insured, owner-operated, Monroe area, and residential/commercial coverage.

4. Services are described in one loose block instead of by task path.
   - Prototype fix: split services into Windows, Solar Panels, Roof and Gutters, and About Ben cards so buyers can find their job faster.

5. The contact flow does not explain what happens after someone reaches out.
   - Prototype fix: add a simple three-step section for send details, get a quote, and schedule the work.

### Medium
6. Reviews and contact details are fine, but the mobile flow can be easier to scan.
   - Prototype fix: shorten review copy, stack sections cleanly, and keep the form block compact.

## Best Page Profit Check improvements
- Keep the same calm white and green local-service feel.
- Clean up all template leftovers.
- Make the top message specific, local, and action-led.
- Move trust signals near the first CTA.
- Group services by what the customer actually needs done.
- Add a short what happens next section.
- Preserve the owner story and reviews, but format them for faster reading.

## SEO basics to tighten
- Use a stronger title tag with Monroe and the core services.
- Make the H1 and first paragraph match the actual jobs.
- Add Monroe, WA and nearby service wording in the hero and service sections.
- Use descriptive image alt text for the real work.
- Keep phone and email links clean and crawlable.
- Add clearer service keywords for windows, screens, solar panels, roof cleaning, and gutter cleaning.

## Realistic implementation angle
This looks like a light Wix cleanup, not a rebuild. A realistic ROM is 3 to 6 hours in Wix if the page structure is kept and only the hero, trust strip, services, copy, and contact flow are tightened. If rebuilt as a custom static page using the same content, it is closer to 1 business day.

## Offer fit
Strong fit for a $99 Page Profit Check. The site already has proof and a real local service, but it leaks trust through unfinished structure and vague top-of-page messaging. This is exactly the kind of cleanup that can lift quote requests without changing the business.
