# Syverson Pressure Washing LLC - Page Profit Check Audit

## Quick take
Syverson already has the bones of a useful homepage: real branding, a relevant pressure-washing hero photo, a service list, a Jobber quote link, phone/email, and at least one strong customer quote. The biggest opportunity is to make the first screen immediately say "Rochester pressure washing," show that the work is surface-safe, add proof above the fold, and make the quote path feel faster than a generic contact click.

## What is already working
- The existing logo is memorable and local-service appropriate.
- The hero image is relevant and looks like actual exterior cleaning work.
- The homepage includes concrete, house wash, windows, gutter cleaning, concrete sealing, decks, fences, holiday lighting, and more.
- The site already links to a Jobber quote request, which is a good conversion destination.
- Phone and email are present: 507-990-9251 and SyversonPressureWashingllc@gmail.com.
- A real testimonial is visible: Hannah Morehart says Isaiah made long-neglected gutters look brand new and gives a 10/10 recommendation.
- Facebook, Instagram, and Google review links are present in the contact area.

## Biggest conversion issues
- The Wix promotional banner at the top makes the site feel unfinished before visitors see the business.
- The hero headline is broad: "superior cleaning solutions" does not quickly communicate pressure washing in Rochester, MN.
- The first screen lists services, but does not explain which surfaces are protected with soft washing or lower-pressure methods.
- Proof is buried below the fold instead of helping the first CTA feel safe.
- "CONTACT US" and "GET A FREE QUOTE!" point to the same quote flow, so one could become a clearer primary action and the other could support phone contact.
- The contact form is long and visually heavy, which can make the quote path feel like work.
- Several service names appear as separate H1s, which is poor SEO structure and makes the page less focused.

## Best Page Profit Check improvements
- Replace the Wix ad space with a simple local trust strip: Rochester, MN, surrounding areas, free estimates, surface-safe cleaning.
- Rewrite the hero H1 to target search intent: "Rochester Pressure Washing That Protects Every Surface."
- Add quick proof in the hero: "10/10 customer recommendation," "Jobber quote request," and "concrete, siding, gutters, decks, fences."
- Keep the original hero photo and logo so the page still feels like Syverson.
- Turn the service list into clearer surface-safe service cards or grouped bullets.
- Move one testimonial near the CTA so visitors see social proof before deciding.
- Add a faster quote card: choose service, enter address, call or start the Jobber request.
- Shorten the form section visually and emphasize phone, email, and the quote request link.

## SEO basics to tighten
- Use one H1 focused on location and service, for example: "Rochester MN Pressure Washing and Soft Washing."
- Add a meta description that mentions Rochester, concrete cleaning, house washing, gutters, decks, fences, and free quotes.
- Rename image alt text from template-like file names to useful descriptions such as "Syverson pressure washing driveway in Rochester MN."
- Add visible service-area language for Rochester and surrounding communities.
- Mark up phone, email, business name, and city consistently in the footer.
- Consider LocalBusiness schema later, especially if the Google review link is active.

## Realistic implementation angle
This can be handled surgically inside the current Wix-style page. No replatform is needed for the first pass. Reuse the existing logo and pressure washing photo, keep the same header/hero/about/testimonial/contact order, and change the copy, CTA hierarchy, proof placement, form emphasis, and SEO basics.

## Offer fit
This is a good fit for a $99 Page Profit Check because the diagnosis is easy to show visually: the current page already looks legitimate, but the first screen misses the local pressure-washing message and the fastest quote path. Implementation should be separate from the $99 audit.
