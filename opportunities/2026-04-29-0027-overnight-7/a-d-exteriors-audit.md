# A&D Exteriors — Page Profit Check Audit

## Quick take
A&D Exteriors is a good fit for a **Solution A surgical homepage cleanup** because the current site already has recognizable local-service proof, brand material, and a reachable contact path — but the first-screen decision flow can be made more direct without making the business feel redesigned.

**Recommendation:** preserve the current site family and rebuild the homepage around one clear primary action: **Request a Quote**.

**Visual resemblance:** High  
**Audit coverage target:** High

## Site fingerprint
- **Logo/brand assets:** Business-name treatment preserved in a cleaner header.
- **Dominant colors:** #174d79, #f08a24, #f4f8fb, #102a43.
- **Typography feel:** Practical local-service sans-serif body copy with stronger display headings for hierarchy.
- **Header/nav pattern:** Contact-forward local service navigation with fewer competing choices.
- **Hero/image treatment:** Same service-business positioning, but with the main action visible immediately.
- **Section rhythm:** Hero, trust strip, service cards, proof band, process block, contact section.
- **Recognizable quirks to preserve:**
  - blue/orange exterior-contractor identity
  - full-service exterior remodeling breadth
  - family-owned and licensed proof
  - 12 Year No-Leak Workmanship Warranty
  - Naperville address and quote-first intent

## What is already working
- The business has a live website with clear service intent in Naperville / Chicagoland, IL.
- The homepage already communicates a real local service category: roofing, siding, windows, gutters, and home exterior remodeling.
- The site includes direct phone, contact, quote, estimate, appointment, or booking paths.
- The brand has proof points worth preserving: Family-owned local company, Licensed, bonded & insured, 12 Year No-Leak Workmanship Warranty.
- The service range is broad enough to support multiple buyer-intent paths.

## Biggest conversion issues

1. **Critical:** The homepage has a quote button, but service, project, blog, and footer density make the quote path less decisive than it should be.
   - Prototype response: Make “Request a Quote” the dominant first-screen action.

2. **High:** Licensed, bonded, insured, family-owned, and warranty proof deserve to be grouped near the first CTA.
   - Prototype response: Group license, family-owned, warranty, and project proof directly under the hero.

3. **High:** Recent project proof is valuable but should support the quote decision rather than compete with the service story.
   - Prototype response: Condense the four main exterior service paths into easy cards.

4. **High:** Long footer/location/service lists make a polished local contractor feel heavier than the core offer.
   - Prototype response: Use a short “what happens next” section before the contact block.

5. **Medium:** The contact form asks useful fields but appears after a lot of scrolling.
   - Prototype response: Keep project/gallery proof visible without letting it overwhelm the quote flow.

## Best Page Profit Check improvements
1. Make “Request a Quote” the dominant first-screen action.
2. Group license, family-owned, warranty, and project proof directly under the hero.
3. Condense the four main exterior service paths into easy cards.
4. Use a short “what happens next” section before the contact block.
5. Keep project/gallery proof visible without letting it overwhelm the quote flow.

## SEO basics to tighten
- Use a homepage title that combines primary service + location + brand.
- Write a concise meta description around the main service and CTA.
- Keep one clear H1 and use H2s for service categories.
- Add descriptive alt text for brand and service images if implemented in the live CMS.
- Add LocalBusiness/Service schema after the approved copy is finalized.

## Realistic implementation angle
This is a focused homepage cleanup sprint, not a rebrand. The existing site should keep its recognizable look while tightening the top section, CTA hierarchy, service grouping, trust proof, and contact expectations.
