# Naperville House Cleaning Service — Page Profit Check Audit

## Quick take
Naperville House Cleaning Service has recognizable local-business value and enough existing proof to justify a **Solution A surgical homepage cleanup** rather than a redesign. The opportunity is to preserve the current visual family while making the first screen, trust proof, service choices, and next-step path easier for a first-time buyer to understand.

**Recommendation:** keep the site’s current brand cues and rebuild the homepage hierarchy around one clear CTA: **Get My Free Quote**.

**Visual resemblance:** High  
**Audit coverage target:** High

## Site fingerprint
- **Logo/brand assets:** Text-based brand treatment preserved with similar local-business styling.
- **Dominant colors:** #31708e, #6db3c8, #f4fbfd, with dark text/contrast.
- **Typography feel:** Roboto, Arial, sans-serif with Roboto, Arial, sans-serif heading feel.
- **Header/nav pattern:** horizontal local-business navigation with phone/contact emphasis.
- **Hero/image treatment:** image-led hero using the same industry/brand direction.
- **Section rhythm:** service cards, trust proof, process/next-step block, contact section.
- **Recognizable quirks to preserve:** Weebly-style local cleaning page with blue/gray tones, Roboto type, top nav, search, repeated call headings, long detailed cleaning checklists, and a large quote form.

## What is already working
- The business has a live website with real local-service intent.
- The site already contains useful proof: Naperville-based cleaner, Detailed bathroom and kitchen checklists, Free quote form.
- Contact information is available and the business can receive direct inquiries.
- The existing visual direction is recognizable enough to preserve instead of replacing.

## Biggest conversion issues

1. **Critical:** The hero uses long SEO-style headings instead of a clear homeowner promise.
   - Prototype response: Rewrite the first screen around detailed Naperville house cleaning and free quote CTA.
2. **High:** The page repeats phone CTAs but does not quickly explain the quote steps.
   - Prototype response: Add a simple three-step free-quote process near the top.
3. **High:** Long checklist copy overwhelms before visitors understand service packages.
   - Prototype response: Group cleaning into recurring, rotational detailed, and move/special quote paths.
4. **Critical:** Some phone links use nonstandard “tell://” formatting in the captured markup.
   - Prototype response: Use clean tel: links and a consistent button label.
5. **Medium:** Navigation includes older COVID concern prominence that may not be a top current buyer need.
   - Prototype response: Keep policy info lower; prioritize reviews, service area, and quote path.

## Best Page Profit Check improvements
1. Replace scattered first-screen messaging with a clear buyer promise: “Detailed residential cleaning in Naperville with a clear free-quote path.”
2. Make **Get My Free Quote** the primary action in the hero, process section, and contact block.
3. Move trust proof near the top: Naperville-based cleaner, Detailed bathroom and kitchen checklists, Free quote form, Recurring and rotational cleaning.
4. Organize services by buyer intent instead of making visitors decode every page/link.
5. Add a simple “what happens next” flow before the form.
6. Clean template residue, duplicate text, stale content, or technical form clutter where present.

## SEO basics to tighten
- Use a homepage title that combines primary service + Naperville/local area + brand.
- Write a concise meta description around the main service and CTA.
- Keep one clear H1 and use H2s for service categories.
- Add alt text for primary service images and logo assets.
- Add LocalBusiness/Service schema after the approved copy is finalized.

## Realistic implementation angle
This is a strong fit for a focused homepage cleanup sprint. The existing site does not need to be reinvented; it needs a cleaner top section, clearer CTA hierarchy, tighter service grouping, more visible trust, and a contact section that tells visitors what will happen next.
