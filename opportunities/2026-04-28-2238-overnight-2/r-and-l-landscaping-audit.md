# R & L Landscaping — Page Profit Check Audit

## Quick take
R & L Landscaping has recognizable local-business value and enough existing proof to justify a **Solution A surgical homepage cleanup** rather than a redesign. The opportunity is to preserve the current visual family while making the first screen, trust proof, service choices, and next-step path easier for a first-time buyer to understand.

**Recommendation:** keep the site’s current brand cues and rebuild the homepage hierarchy around one clear CTA: **Request a Free Quote**.

**Visual resemblance:** High  
**Audit coverage target:** High

## Site fingerprint
- **Logo/brand assets:** Text-based brand treatment preserved with similar local-business styling.
- **Dominant colors:** #6a8f36, #a5c654, #f4f7ed, with dark text/contrast.
- **Typography feel:** Raleway, Arial, sans-serif with Raleway, Arial, sans-serif heading feel.
- **Header/nav pattern:** horizontal local-business navigation with phone/contact emphasis.
- **Hero/image treatment:** image-led hero using the same industry/brand direction.
- **Section rhythm:** service cards, trust proof, process/next-step block, contact section.
- **Recognizable quirks to preserve:** Older WordPress landscaping site with Raleway, green lawn imagery, centered slider copy, residential/commercial service buttons, testimonial slider, and quote form.

## What is already working
- The business has a live website with real local-service intent.
- The site already contains useful proof: Family owned and operated, Serving since 1999, Residential + commercial crews.
- Contact information is available and the business can receive direct inquiries.
- The existing visual direction is recognizable enough to preserve instead of replacing.

## Biggest conversion issues

1. **High:** The homepage has a dated slider-style structure with several broad claims before a direct quote path.
   - Prototype response: Create one clear hero with Request a Free Quote CTA and service-division buttons.
2. **Critical:** Request Quote appears in nav but opens/duplicates form behavior that can feel unclear.
   - Prototype response: Make quote CTA consistently point to a concise contact/quote section.
3. **High:** Service divisions are helpful but buried after long welcome copy.
   - Prototype response: Move residential/commercial/full-service cards closer to the top.
4. **High:** Trust proof since 1999 and family-owned status is strong but not visually summarized.
   - Prototype response: Add trust strip under hero with tenure, family-owned, service area, and client type.
5. **Critical:** Contact forms show technical residue/duplicate form fields in capture.
   - Prototype response: Clean the prototype form labels and remove duplicate/script residue.

## Best Page Profit Check improvements
1. Replace scattered first-screen messaging with a clear buyer promise: “High-quality landscape services for Naperville and the Chicagoland suburbs.”
2. Make **Request a Free Quote** the primary action in the hero, process section, and contact block.
3. Move trust proof near the top: Family owned and operated, Serving since 1999, Residential + commercial crews, Free estimate by phone or form.
4. Organize services by buyer intent instead of making visitors decode every page/link.
5. Add a simple “what happens next” flow before the form.
6. Clean template residue, duplicate text, stale content, or technical form clutter where present.

## SEO basics to tighten
- Use a homepage title that combines primary service + Naperville/local area + brand.
- Write a concise meta description around the main service and CTA.
- Keep one clear H1 and use H2s for service categories.
- Add alt text for primary service images and logo assets.
- Add LocalBusiness/Service schema after the approved copy is finalized.

## Realistic implementation angle
This is a strong fit for a focused homepage cleanup sprint. The existing site does not need to be reinvented; it needs a cleaner top section, clearer CTA hierarchy, tighter service grouping, more visible trust, and a contact section that tells visitors what will happen next.
