# Mikes Landscaping Service — Page Profit Check Audit

## Quick take
Mikes Landscaping Service has recognizable local-business value and enough existing proof to justify a **Solution A surgical homepage cleanup** rather than a redesign. The opportunity is to preserve the current visual family while making the first screen, trust proof, service choices, and next-step path easier for a first-time buyer to understand.

**Recommendation:** keep the site’s current brand cues and rebuild the homepage hierarchy around one clear CTA: **Request a Free Estimate**.

**Visual resemblance:** High  
**Audit coverage target:** High

## Site fingerprint
- **Logo/brand assets:** Text-based brand treatment preserved with similar local-business styling.
- **Dominant colors:** #2f6f3e, #d69a2d, #f5f1e7, with dark text/contrast.
- **Typography feel:** Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif with Georgia, Times, serif heading feel.
- **Header/nav pattern:** horizontal local-business navigation with phone/contact emphasis.
- **Hero/image treatment:** image-led hero using the same industry/brand direction.
- **Section rhythm:** service cards, trust proof, process/next-step block, contact section.
- **Recognizable quirks to preserve:** Long Weebly/contractor page with green landscaping imagery, serif headline moments, many uppercase CTAs, quote form, service tiles, and extended FAQ sections.

## What is already working
- The business has a live website with real local-service intent.
- The site already contains useful proof: Family-owned and operated, 3D design planning available, Free estimates.
- Contact information is available and the business can receive direct inquiries.
- The existing visual direction is recognizable enough to preserve instead of replacing.

## Biggest conversion issues

1. **Critical:** Multiple competing H1-style location headlines make the first screen feel repetitive and less polished.
   - Prototype response: Use one clear Naperville/Aurora landscaping headline and keep location coverage in supporting copy.
2. **High:** The quote path exists but is split among several “Learn More” and estimate buttons.
   - Prototype response: Make “Request a Free Estimate” the primary CTA in hero, nav, process, and footer.
3. **High:** Service depth is strong but overwhelming when presented as a long list.
   - Prototype response: Reframe services into outdoor living, landscape solutions, and hardscape details.
4. **Critical:** The FAQ includes unrelated wording about Colorado, which can damage local trust.
   - Prototype response: Remove mismatched location copy and replace with local-service-area proof.
5. **Medium:** Trust values and process are useful but arrive after too much repetition.
   - Prototype response: Bring values and four-step process closer to the top.

## Best Page Profit Check improvements
1. Replace scattered first-screen messaging with a clear buyer promise: “Naperville landscaping, hardscaping, and outdoor living — from quote to install.”
2. Make **Request a Free Estimate** the primary action in the hero, process section, and contact block.
3. Move trust proof near the top: Family-owned and operated, 3D design planning available, Free estimates, Naperville, Aurora & suburbs.
4. Organize services by buyer intent instead of making visitors decode every page/link.
5. Add a simple “what happens next” flow before the form.
6. Clean template residue, duplicate text, stale content, or technical form clutter where present.

## SEO basics to tighten
- Use a homepage title that combines primary service + Naperville/local area + brand.
- Write a concise meta description around the main service and CTA.
- Keep one clear H1 and use H2s for service categories.
- Add alt text for primary service images and logo assets.
- Add LocalBusiness/Service schema after the approved copy is finalized.

## Realistic implementation angle
This is a strong fit for a focused homepage cleanup sprint. The existing site does not need to be reinvented; it needs a cleaner top section, clearer CTA hierarchy, tighter service grouping, more visible trust, and a contact section that tells visitors what will happen next.
