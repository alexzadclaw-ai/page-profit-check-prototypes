# Morlock Landscape and Design — Page Profit Check Audit

## Quick take
Morlock Landscape and Design has recognizable local-business value and enough existing proof to justify a **Solution A surgical homepage cleanup** rather than a redesign. The opportunity is to preserve the current visual family while making the first screen, trust proof, service choices, and next-step path easier for a first-time buyer to understand.

**Recommendation:** keep the site’s current brand cues and rebuild the homepage hierarchy around one clear CTA: **Schedule a Free Estimate**.

**Visual resemblance:** High  
**Audit coverage target:** High

## Site fingerprint
- **Logo/brand assets:** Existing logo/wordmark preserved in the prototype.
- **Dominant colors:** #336e3c, #18d5d7, #f4f0e8, with dark text/contrast.
- **Typography feel:** Source Sans Pro, Arial, sans-serif with Raleway, Arial, sans-serif heading feel.
- **Header/nav pattern:** horizontal local-business navigation with phone/contact emphasis.
- **Hero/image treatment:** image-led hero using the same industry/brand direction.
- **Section rhythm:** service cards, trust proof, process/next-step block, contact section.
- **Recognizable quirks to preserve:** Hearst/Duda-style local contractor site; white header with green phone, dark/green landscape palette, bold uppercase nav, large photo/service tiles, Source Sans/Raleway feel.

## What is already working
- The business has a live website with real local-service intent.
- The site already contains useful proof: 35+ years of experience, Licensed, bonded & insured, Founder-led local service.
- Contact information is available and the business can receive direct inquiries.
- The existing visual direction is recognizable enough to preserve instead of replacing.

## Biggest conversion issues

1. **Critical:** The hero is mostly a slogan, so the first screen does not quickly separate design, build, maintenance, and drainage buyers.
   - Prototype response: Rewrite the hero around “landscaping and outdoor living in Naperville” with estimate and phone CTAs.
2. **High:** Important trust proof is buried inside paragraphs instead of visible near the top.
   - Prototype response: Add a four-point trust strip under the hero: 35+ years, licensed/bonded/insured, founder-led, local service area.
3. **High:** The service menu is broad and hard to scan for first-time visitors.
   - Prototype response: Group services into three buyer-intent cards: design/install, outdoor living/hardscape, maintenance/drainage.
4. **High:** The form appears only after a lot of copy and includes several fields before expectations are set.
   - Prototype response: Move a concise free-estimate panel and three-step process higher on the page.
5. **Medium:** Long seasonal copy can distract from evergreen high-value projects.
   - Prototype response: Keep seasonal cleanup as a service, but prioritize year-round project categories.

## Best Page Profit Check improvements
1. Replace scattered first-screen messaging with a clear buyer promise: “Family-owned landscaping and outdoor living in Naperville.”
2. Make **Schedule a Free Estimate** the primary action in the hero, process section, and contact block.
3. Move trust proof near the top: 35+ years of experience, Licensed, bonded & insured, Founder-led local service, Naperville + nearby suburbs.
4. Organize services by buyer intent instead of making visitors decode every page/link.
5. Add a simple “what happens next” flow before the form.
6. Clean template residue, duplicate text, stale content, or technical form clutter where present.

## SEO basics to tighten
- Use a homepage title that combines primary service + Naperville/local area + brand.
- Write a concise meta description around the main service and CTA.
- Keep one clear H1 and use H2s for service categories.
- Add alt text for primary service images and logo assets.
- Add LocalBusiness/Service schema after the approved copy is finalized.

## Realistic implementation angle
This is a strong fit for a focused homepage cleanup sprint. The existing site does not need to be reinvented; it needs a cleaner top section, clearer CTA hierarchy, tighter service grouping, more visible trust, and a contact section that tells visitors what will happen next.
