# Sol Rejuvenation Therapeutic Massage — Page Profit Check Audit

## Quick take
Sol Rejuvenation Therapeutic Massage has recognizable local-business value and enough existing proof to justify a **Solution A surgical homepage cleanup** rather than a redesign. The opportunity is to preserve the current visual family while making the first screen, trust proof, service choices, and next-step path easier for a first-time buyer to understand.

**Recommendation:** keep the site’s current brand cues and rebuild the homepage hierarchy around one clear CTA: **Book a Massage**.

**Visual resemblance:** High  
**Audit coverage target:** High

## Site fingerprint
- **Logo/brand assets:** Text-based brand treatment preserved with similar local-business styling.
- **Dominant colors:** #8f6e6e, #c6a47d, #fbf7f2, with dark text/contrast.
- **Typography feel:** Raleway, Arial, sans-serif with Sacramento, Georgia, serif heading feel.
- **Header/nav pattern:** horizontal local-business navigation with phone/contact emphasis.
- **Hero/image treatment:** image-led hero using the same industry/brand direction.
- **Section rhythm:** service cards, trust proof, process/next-step block, contact section.
- **Recognizable quirks to preserve:** GoDaddy wellness site with soft beige/rose-brown palette, Raleway body text, script-style headings, stock spa imagery, long one-page service menu, and contact/hours block.

## What is already working
- The business has a live website with real local-service intent.
- The site already contains useful proof: Licensed massage therapy experience since 2001, Aromatherapy included, Foot soak + warm towels available.
- Contact information is available and the business can receive direct inquiries.
- The existing visual direction is recognizable enough to preserve instead of replacing.

## Biggest conversion issues

1. **Critical:** The first screen is generic and quickly followed by “My Blog / posts coming soon,” which weakens trust.
   - Prototype response: Replace the blog-first flow with a clear therapeutic-massage hero, trust strip, and booking CTA.
2. **High:** Services are extremely detailed but hard to scan.
   - Prototype response: Group modalities into customized massage, specialty bodywork, and wellness add-ons.
3. **Critical:** Old 2018 grand-opening/news copy makes the site feel stale.
   - Prototype response: Remove dated event/news blocks from the homepage prototype and keep evergreen service content.
4. **High:** Booking instructions are split between “Book Now,” contact form, and text note.
   - Prototype response: Create a simple three-step booking flow emphasizing text scheduling and online booking.
5. **Medium:** Mission/owner proof is buried far down the page.
   - Prototype response: Bring licensed-experience and Naperville tenure proof into the top half.

## Best Page Profit Check improvements
1. Replace scattered first-screen messaging with a clear buyer promise: “Therapeutic massage and holistic bodywork in Naperville.”
2. Make **Book a Massage** the primary action in the hero, process section, and contact block.
3. Move trust proof near the top: Licensed massage therapy experience since 2001, Aromatherapy included, Foot soak + warm towels available, Text scheduling recommended.
4. Organize services by buyer intent instead of making visitors decode every page/link.
5. Add a simple “what happens next” flow before the form.
6. Clean template residue, duplicate text, stale content, or technical form clutter where present.

## SEO basics to tighten
- Use a homepage title that combines primary service + Naperville/local area + brand.
- Write a concise meta description around the main service and CTA.
- Keep one clear H1 and use H2s for service categories.
- Add alt text for primary service images and logo assets.
- Add LocalBusiness/Service schema after the approved copy is finalized.

## Realistic implementation angle
This is a strong fit for a focused homepage cleanup sprint. The existing site does not need to be reinvented; it needs a cleaner top section, clearer CTA hierarchy, tighter service grouping, more visible trust, and a contact section that tells visitors what will happen next.
