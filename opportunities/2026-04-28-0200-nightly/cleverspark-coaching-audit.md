## Quick take

CleverSpark Coaching has a personable voice, a broad-enough coaching offer, and pricing that feels accessible. Kimberly’s story is also a real asset: counseling background, higher-ed experience, first-generation context, and lived ADHD perspective all create genuine trust.

Right now, though, the site makes visitors work too hard to understand the offers.

The main issues are:
- repeated navigation / template clutter
- several visible typos that weaken trust
- long service blocks that are hard to scan
- limited proof framing near key CTAs
- weak CTA sequencing between “learn,” “compare packages,” and “book”
- contact page that feels thin at the exact moment reassurance matters most

That makes this a very pitchable **$99 mini-audit + optional cleanup sprint** lead: the site is not broken, but it likely leaks consults from friction that is easy to explain and realistic to fix.

## What is already working

- Warm, human founder-led positioning
- Clear themes: career transitions, neurodivergence, workplace support, education goals
- Pricing is visible and comparatively approachable
- Package naming gives the brand some personality
- Kimberly’s backstory adds authenticity and relatability
- There is already a free consult CTA, so the business is oriented around conversation-first conversion
- Calendly is present, which is good for low-friction booking

## Biggest conversion issues

### 1) Tighten first-screen clarity
The homepage opens with a friendly introduction, but the message is broad and the value prop is not yet crisp.

A new visitor should understand within a few seconds:
- who this is for
- what kinds of problems get solved
- why Kimberly is a credible guide
- what the next step is

**Better direction:**
Use a headline/subheadline combination that names the ideal client more directly, such as career changers, job seekers, neurodivergent professionals, and students needing direction.

### 2) Clean up trust leaks from typos and formatting
There are multiple visible issues that chip away at trust:
- “CleverSpark Coacghing” in footer areas
- “Resume Wtiring”
- “Caoching Bundle”
- “generaly”
- some sections run together without spacing

For a coaching business selling clarity and support, these details matter more than they would on a rough early-stage hobby site.

### 3) Make packages far easier to compare
The service page has useful information, but each package currently reads like a dense paragraph.

**Improvement direction:**
Turn each package into a clean card with:
- who it is for
- session count
- deliverables
- price
- payment option
- primary CTA

This would materially improve mobile scannability and help visitors self-sort faster.

### 4) Improve CTA hierarchy and sequencing
The current experience repeats “Work with Me” / “Book Consultation” but does not always guide the visitor through a clear sequence.

**Better sequence:**
1. understand the problem and fit
2. compare package paths
3. book a free consult

A stronger homepage would use one primary CTA consistently, with secondary links for people who still need details.

### 5) Surface founder credibility earlier
Kimberly’s strongest trust builders are meaningful, but much of that detail sits on the About page.

**Easy win:**
Add a compact credibility band near the top of the homepage with cues like:
- 15+ years supporting students and professionals
- Master’s in Mental Health Counseling
- Higher-ed and private coaching experience
- ADHD-informed coaching perspective

That makes the consult CTA feel safer and more earned.

### 6) Clarify who each offer is for
The current packages are branded, but a visitor still has to interpret which one matches their situation.

**Example framing:**
- **Career clarity:** for people exploring what comes next
- **Job search:** for resumes, interviews, and strategy
- **Growth/pivot:** for professionals making a deliberate next move
- **Networking/LinkedIn:** for visibility and connection-building
- **Student support:** for school + career planning
- **Neurodivergent support:** for ADHD, burnout, energy, and self-advocacy

This is especially important because there are several packages at similar price points.

### 7) Strengthen proof framing without overclaiming
The site feels warm, but it does not yet make enough use of outcome-oriented reassurance.

Good additions would include:
- a short “what clients usually come in needing help with” section
- 2–4 lightweight testimonials or anonymized wins
- a brief “what happens in a consult” explainer

Even without adding heavy social proof, better framing can make the service feel more tangible.

### 8) Make the contact page do more conversion work
The contact page mostly says “fill out the information below.” That is functional, but thin.

**Improvement direction:**
Add a reassurance block near the form:
- free 30-minute consultation
- no pressure to commit on the call
- ideal for choosing the right package or building a custom one
- follow-up by email after submission

### 9) Support SEO basics more intentionally
This does not need a major SEO campaign, but the page structure could better support search visibility.

Useful cleanup:
- clearer H1/H2 structure
- distinct page titles and descriptions for homepage/services/contact
- more explicit phrases around career coaching, ADHD coaching, job search support, and student guidance
- FAQ content that mirrors real search questions

## Why this is a good Page Profit Check lead

This is a strong fit because the offer is already believable and affordable. The business does not need a dramatic redesign pitch. It needs a sensible cleanup story:

- less friction
- cleaner trust signals
- easier package comparison
- stronger CTA flow
- better mobile readability

That is exactly the kind of before/after story a mini-audit can show quickly.

For this batch, the prototype should:
- keep the warm, approachable tone
- sharpen the homepage headline and subheadline
- add an early credibility / trust strip

- include a “who this is for” section
- show a simple 3-step consultation process
- add a small FAQ block
- strengthen the final CTA and contact reassurance
