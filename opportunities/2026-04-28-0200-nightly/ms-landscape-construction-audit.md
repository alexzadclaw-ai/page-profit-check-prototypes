## Summary

This business has a solid underlying offer: 20+ years of experience, visually tangible outdoor projects, multiple service categories, and a local-service footprint that can support high-value quote requests. The problem is not the service itself — it is the website presentation.

Right now the site creates friction with:
- cluttered and repetitive navigation
- visible template leftovers and irrelevant links
- awkward grammar that weakens trust
- weak CTA hierarchy
- limited proof near the decision point
- inconsistent service naming and page structure

That combination makes the page feel less established than the company likely is. A polished, quote-first landing page could make the business look more credible immediately without changing the core offer.

## What’s already working

1. **Clear local-service category** with strong homeowner intent
2. **20+ years of experience** mentioned on the homepage
3. **Several service lines** that are easy to group into hardscapes, landscape design, decks/fences, and home improvements
4. **Direct phone contact** already present on the site
5. **Portfolio/gallery angle** that can support trust once presented more cleanly

## Biggest conversion problems

### 1) The top of the site does not behave like a quote page
The homepage hero says “we make your dream come true,” but it does not quickly answer:
- what they build
- where they work
- why homeowners should trust them
- what to do next

A stronger hero should lead with the practical offer, not a generic slogan.

**Recommended direction:**
> Custom hardscapes, landscape design, decks, and fences for homeowners in Rockland, Orange, Dutchess, and Ulster County — built with 20+ years of hands-on experience.

Follow that with a simple quote CTA and 2–3 trust cues.

### 2) Trust drops because of template residue and grammar issues
The current site includes several visible credibility leaks:
- duplicate navigation blocks
- irrelevant links like Volunteer / Donate
- footer links pointing to template/demo-style destinations
- misspellings and awkward phrasing like “DEKCES” and “Whatever it is, the way you tell your story online can make all the difference.”

For a local construction company, this can make visitors hesitate before calling.

### 3) CTA hierarchy is weak and inconsistent
The site has contact info, but it does not consistently guide visitors toward one main action. A local-service page like this should make one action dominant:
- **Primary:** Call or request a quote
- **Secondary:** View project photos / service categories

Right now the user has to work too hard to understand what the next step should be.

### 4) Services are present, but not organized around buyer intent
The content mentions hardscapes, landscape design, decks, fences, and home improvement, but the structure is noisy. A cleaner page should group services into scannable cards with short benefits and a quote CTA beneath them.

### 5) There is not enough visible proof near the top
The site references years of experience and shows a service area, but the proof is buried. Stronger trust framing should appear early:
- 20+ years of experience
- named service area counties
- project category list
- direct phone number
- short process for estimates

If available later, testimonials or more project-specific before/after images would help even more.

## Specific page cleanup opportunities

### Hero / above-the-fold
Replace the generic slogan with a concrete local-service headline.

Include:
- service categories
- counties served
- phone number
- “Request a Quote” CTA
- “View Projects” secondary CTA

### Navigation cleanup
The nav should be drastically simplified.

Suggested top-level nav:
- Services
- Project Gallery
- About
- Service Area
- Contact

Remove or hide links that weaken trust, especially:
- Volunteer
- Donate
- template/demo leftovers
- repeated duplicate menu sets

### Services section
Turn the service list into 4 short cards:
- Hardscapes
- Landscape Design
- Decks & Fences
- Home Improvements

Each card should explain the outcome for the homeowner, not just list materials.

### Quote process block
Add a short “How it works” section:
1. Call or request a quote
2. Discuss goals, layout, and budget
3. Get a clear plan for your project

This reduces hesitation for homeowners who are still comparing vendors.

### Trust section
Add a compact proof band near the hero and a fuller trust section lower on the page:
- 20+ years of experience
- local counties served
- direct owner contact
- project gallery / portfolio access

### SEO basics
The site would benefit from foundational local SEO cleanup:
- clearer page titles for service pages
- consistent H1/H2 hierarchy
- stronger location terms on the homepage
- service-specific copy that matches buyer searches like hardscape contractor, fence installation, deck builder, landscape design
- cleaner internal linking between services and contact page
