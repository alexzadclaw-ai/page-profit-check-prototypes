What the notes say

Each annotation names a visible issue and why it affects conversion.

1 · First impression

Duplicate navigation makes the page feel templated

The stacked menus create friction before the offer even lands. Trimming this to one clean nav would immediately make the business feel more intentional.

2 · Mid-page proof

Google review area is under-explained

There is trust here, but it’s visually soft. A tighter review strip or clearer testimonial module would help visitors connect the promise to social proof.

3 · Main body copy

Good substance, but too dense to scan fast

The experience and benefits are strong. Breaking this into shorter chunks and scannable bullets would preserve the voice while increasing comprehension.

4 · Conversion form

“New Field” labels damage trust right before inquiry

This is the biggest credibility leak. It reads like unfinished software rather than a premium training business, right at the moment someone considers contacting them.

5 · Footer / leftovers

Roofing links reveal a template artifact

This is the clearest proof that the page was never fully cleaned up. Annotating it visually makes the value of a conversion review obvious in seconds.

Does this solve the trust problem better than a full redesign prototype?

Yes, for trust and conversation flow
  • The business owner sees their real page immediately, so the critique feels grounded.
  • The value proposition becomes diagnostic: fix leaks, don’t replace identity.
  • It avoids accidental overpromising from a polished mockup the client may assume includes a full rebuild.
  • It makes “why should I believe you?” much easier to answer visually.
But less persuasive for pure wow-factor
  • It will not create the same emotional lift as a dramatic before/after redesign.
  • Some clients respond more strongly to aspiration than diagnosis.
  • It works best when the sale is a conversion review or cleanup engagement, not a premium rebrand.
Recommendation: use Solution B when the main obstacle is disbelief or defensiveness. Use full redesign prototypes only when the client already trusts the process and needs help imagining a larger repositioning.
